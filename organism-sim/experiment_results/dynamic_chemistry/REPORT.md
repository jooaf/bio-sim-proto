# Dynamic chemistry study report

## Protocol

The study used `experiments/run_dynamic_chemistry.nu` with five matched seeds (100–104), 300 founders, 8 founder archetypes, 8 elements, 48 molecules, and 4,000 ticks per run. All conditions enabled cellular affordances so transient internal guests could occur. The common guest stress was deliberately permissive (`emergence_engulfment_rate=1.0`, `emergence_exchange_rate=0.10`, and `emergence_module_cost=0.001`).

| Condition | Seasons | Dynamic chemistry |
|---|---:|---:|
| baseline | off | off |
| seasons | on | off |
| dynamic | off | on |
| both | on | on |

Dynamic runs used 6 bounded element-conserving reaction rules, environmental reaction rate 0.04, reaction thermodynamics 0.10, byproduct strength 0.05, byproduct decay 0.02, and both chemistry couplings 0.75. Each run wrote a schema-3 compact Rust recording. The per-run and aggregate CSV files in this directory are generated by the runner.

`dynamic_chemistry_enabled` reserves a deterministic closure of simple dimer compounds in the dynamic catalog. Consequently, the matched-seed comparison isolates the ecological condition operationally, but dynamic/off runs do not have identical molecule catalogs; this is recorded as a limitation rather than treated as a randomized controlled comparison.

## Interpretation

- **Dynamic chemistry was active:** dynamic runs produced roughly 1,000 environmental reactions, tens of thousands of byproduct emissions, and more than 13,000 occupied sparse byproduct positions by the final tick. This verifies temporal/spatial chemical state change rather than a dormant code path.
- **No extinction regression in this horizon:** all four conditions retained a nonzero population. Dynamic and both had lower mean final populations than their respective no-chemistry controls, so the buffering hypothesis was not supported by this protocol.
- **Niche association is inconclusive:** the raw catalyst-minus-toxin regime had nonzero variance under dynamic chemistry, but the species association statistic is unstable at low population and has no baseline counterpart. A longer study should compare species/regime association at matched population windows and use confidence intervals.
- **No endosymbiosis-like advantage was demonstrated:** final-snapshot guests were sparse (dynamic: 4/5 runs had at least one guest host at the final snapshot; both: 1/5), and the guest regime delta was negative in every dynamic run with a final guest host. Cumulative guest exchange exceeded upkeep in absolute terms, but this was also true in the baseline and is not a no-guest causal contrast.
- **Performance:** the recorded mean TPS was 22.8% below baseline for dynamic and 22.8% below baseline for both. A phase-level 4,000-tick profile on the final kernel measured approximately 1,138 TPS baseline versus 1,003 TPS dynamic (11.9% slower); recording/seed variance and the growing sparse map explain why the end-to-end run means are a stricter result. The reaction candidate index and staged writes keep the kernel phase bounded, but further sparse-map compaction or lower-frequency aggregate scans may be needed to meet a 15% end-to-end target.

## Reproducibility and files

```text
nu experiments/run_dynamic_chemistry.nu
nu experiments/run_dynamic_chemistry.nu --analyze-only
```

- `run_summary.csv`: one row per condition/seed with lifecycle, reaction, guest, association, TPS, and conservation fields.
- `group_summary.csv`: five-seed condition means and integrity maxima.
- `performance_profile.csv`: the final-kernel 4,000-tick phase-level baseline/dynamic comparison.
- `runs/<condition>/seed-<seed>/...`: compact JSONL/NPZ Rust records, manifests, and audits.
