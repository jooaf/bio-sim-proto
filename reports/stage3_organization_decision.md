# Stage 3 empirical organization decision

## Decision

- **Composition-reaction identifiability: GO.**
- **Integrated empirical-organization excess: NO-GO.**

## Identifiability

All 15 frozen windows passed:

- approximately 4,565–4,783 sampled reactions/window;
- 89–172 changed reactions/window;
- unique-species fractions 0.060–0.077;
- singleton-species fractions 0.025–0.076;
- recurrent-observation fractions 0.994–0.998.

The eleven-component opcode-count representation therefore avoids the exact-hash singleton failure and supports repeatable reaction-network analysis.

## Organization endpoint

- Mean accepted closed/self-maintaining active candidates: `0.4` per window.
- Accepted candidates appeared in 5/15 windows.
- Product-permutation null accepted none in every permutation.
- Null 95th percentile: `0`.
- Campaign Monte Carlo `p = 0.005`.

Despite exceeding the null, the result failed the preregistered prevalence requirement of accepted candidates in at least 8/15 windows. The integrated endpoint is therefore unsupported.

## Mechanical integrity

- Successful runs: 3/3.
- Exact matter conservation: 3/3.
- Invariant failures: zero.
- Maximum relative energy error: `1.831e-11`.

## Interpretation

The positive identifiability result is useful: opcode-count species are recurrent enough for empirical reaction analysis. The intermittent accepted components are rarer than the frozen minimum and are measured under a degenerate-zero null, so they cannot support a robust organization claim.

No intervention test is launched because the confirmatory organization gate failed. The species definition, windows, sampling rate, and prevalence threshold will not be changed to rescue it.

## Supported claim

> Coarse-grained opcode-composition reactions are statistically identifiable in the selected energy regime, and a small number of empirically balanced components occur more often than product-label permutations, but not with preregistered temporal prevalence.

## Unsupported claims

- persistent chemical organization;
- self-maintenance under intervention;
- organizational closure beyond observed windows;
- organism identity;
- trophic ecology or adaptive selection.
