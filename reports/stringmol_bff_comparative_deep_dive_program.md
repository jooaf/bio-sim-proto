# Prospective Stringmol–BFF comparative deep-dive program

## Question and inference boundary

The program asks which mechanisms explain persistent seeded reproduction in the
tested Stringmol system versus transient functional propagation in the tested
conserved BFF soups. It is not a language ranking. Historical pass rates, births,
depths, mutation probabilities, pool multipliers, lengths, and runtimes are not
directly comparable because opportunities and endpoints differ.

Established observations are narrower:

- AC-P006 and FR-P001 showed sequence-specific transient BFF amplification and
  provenance-linked functional propagation, but every final witness/control
  abundance was zero.
- SM-L001 showed native successful cleavage birth and persistent individual
  lineage over 5,000 ticks.
- SM-C001 retained that result under exact symbol conservation and showed active
  material scarcity; SM-C002 then isolated a causal role for accessible
  decay-released matter.
- SM-H001 established descendant reproductive renewal with inherited-sequence
  continuity. SM-H002's exact-versus-composition-shuffle screen was unevaluable
  because its canonical control split one identical trajectory by founder cohort.
  SM-H003 prospectively repaired that control: five fixed genotypes showed
  replicated sequence-order effects under canonical support, but only 5/14 passed
  versus the frozen 12/14 panel gate. Panel functional heredity and the gated
  catalyst/template follow-up remain unsupported.
- Therefore exact conservation alone cannot explain the historical contrast.
  Global-interaction/global-placement Stringmol also shows that locality is not
  necessary for the demonstrated Stringmol result.

Atomic byte exchange is not atomic offspring creation. BFF has the former but no
native birth event. Stringmol cleavage transfers a suffix into a separately scheduled individual and
creates no net material. Cleavage at offset zero can transfer the complete parent
sequence to a child while destroying the emptied parent, without a preceding copy.
Thus a successful birth—even with a complete child sequence—does not by itself
establish copy-mediated replication or population increase.
Identity labels are observational, while separate storage, scheduling, survival,
and reproductive participation are candidate causal boundaries.

## Common prospective outcome layers

Historical endpoints remain unchanged and are reported separately. New studies
should distinguish:

1. **Functional propagation:** abundance, extinction time, late-window minimum,
   and gains/losses per execution opportunity.
2. **Reproductive renewal:** late-created products that later contribute to
   successful reproduction, after founders are excluded.
3. **Heredity:** causal effect of inherited-sequence perturbation and repair on
   descendant reproductive performance.

Stringmol descendants must additionally be assayed for reproductive competence;
ancestry depth alone can include inert products. BFF must not be assigned
biological parenthood merely because a resident slot was overwritten. Partner-
dependent propagation is legitimate but must state its supplied catalyst/template
context.

## Ordered program

### 1. Measurement, inoculum, and partner context

Within each substrate vary focal frequency and partner composition independently:
compatible replicators, validated inert partners, and frozen heterogeneous
background. In Stringmol vary occupancy separately from focal frequency. Start
with controlled reaction assays in both orientations, then population assays.
Measure encounters, execution cost, template/source damage, functional output,
and serial propagation with standardized partners.

Prediction: if dilution or partner compatibility dominates, BFF persistence
improves in compatible backgrounds and Stringmol deteriorates when compatible
partners are rare. Persistence across overlapping exposure regimes rejects this
as the major explanation.

### 2. Mutation baseline and birth-boundary factorial

First compare mutation-off with native mutation under otherwise identical
mechanics. Then separate:

- offspring separation: independent product versus product retained within or
  overwriting molecular state;
- parent preservation: source modified versus prospectively defined protection;
- admission: vacancy versus replacement at fixed capacity.

Begin with Stringmol ablations around its validated positive control. SM-M001
completed the mutation baseline: native and zero-mutation canonical renewal were
both viable (18/20 each), while the broad realized-divergence gate was unsupported.
The birth-boundary factorial is therefore eligible without claiming equivalent
mutation-arm performance. Then build a BFF-derived experimental substrate in
which products arise from execution, not an automatic copier or retrospective
score-64 admission. Freeze material cost,
completion, parent disposition, and scheduling before outcomes.

Prediction: a birth-boundary explanation requires both loss under Stringmol
ablation and rescue in the BFF-derived variant, beyond parent-protection and
admission effects. Modified BFF is a new substrate, not a reversal of FR-P001.

### 3. Catalyst/template contribution

Use a prospective 2×2 reaction assay crossing competent/perturbed catalyst with
competent/perturbed template, in both orientations. Preserve length, composition,
and binding opportunity where possible and explicitly measure residual binding
differences. Trace actual read source, active/passive role, cleavage target, and
offspring performance with fresh standardized partners.

A division-of-labor explanation requires separable catalytic and template effects
on productive offspring, not merely two ancestry edges.

### 4. Locality, occupancy, and admission

Cross interaction locality with offspring-placement locality (global/global,
local/global, global/local, local/local), then cross selected viable variants with
frozen occupancy and admission levels. Measure opportunities, productive
encounters, placement failure, replacement loss, clustering, and persistence.
Host-only assays precede any parasite extension.

### 5. Scarcity, alphabet support, and recycling

Separate unconserved, conserved nonbinding, and conserved scarce conditions. Keep
inventory equal when inventory is not the treatment. Distinguish histogram versus
uniform symbol support: SM-C001 m16 still permanently excluded symbols absent from
the host. Randomize accessibility after death between reusable pool and conserved
inert waste. SM-C002 is the first experiment in this module.

### 6. Length, workspace, turnover, and horizon

After viable controls exist, vary initial program length, buffer/workspace
capacity, and offspring length independently. Cross selected conditions with
mutation and decay while recording error proposals, accepted changes, resident-
byte exposure, and offspring divergence. Extend successful conditions to a
substantially longer preregistered horizon.

## Statistical and design rules

- The experimental unit is a population run, not a callback, molecule, or birth.
- Block by genotype and environment; BFF witnesses are distinct source genotypes.
- Calibrate intervention viability separately, then freeze unseen confirmatory
  seeds and a minimum meaningful effect.
- Do not default to ten seeds or align runs retrospectively by realized
  generations. Report native time and fixed exposure measures.
- Retain ties and extinctions. A wide null is inconclusive; a precisely small
  effect can reject a candidate as a major contributor.
- Prefer small complete factorials with preregistered interactions over one large
  omnibus screen.
- Preserve observation isolation, deterministic repeats, exact conservation where
  enabled, identity/bounds integrity, and independent reconstruction.

The strongest eventual triangulation is a matched pattern: Stringmol ablation
removes persistent reproductive renewal, a BFF-derived rescue adds it, and both
effects track the same predeclared mechanism. Even that would establish a
conditional mechanism—not spontaneous origin, adaptation, energetic closure,
ecology, organisms, or equivalence of the two languages.
