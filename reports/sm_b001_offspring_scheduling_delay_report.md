## Bounded conclusion

Immediate next-tick reaction eligibility is not required for robust canonical
renewal under the tested conserved, zero-mutation condition: a fixed 500-tick
delay remained viable in 20/20 runs with adequate release exposure. This is not
an equivalence or superiority claim. The delay changes mortality opportunities,
encounters, occupancy, and downstream population composition as part of its total
policy effect.

The descriptive increase in renewal after delay may reflect filtering or changed
encounter/population structure; it does not establish a beneficial scheduling
mechanism, adaptation, selection, or organism-level regulation. Permanently
locking offspring abolishes renewal by policy and cannot establish competence
loss.

The preregistered continuation condition passed. A separate scheduling ×
source-remnant-retention experiment may now be designed. It must retain exact
matter accounting, separate native H001 lineage from transfer lineage, and avoid
claiming that this Stringmol ablation alone explains the BFF contrast.
